A clean 40 MHz ring on a rail that was supposed to be flat DC. Not noise, not a
scope artefact from a bad ground lead — a genuine, repeatable oscillation that
appeared the moment the regulator came out of shutdown.

## what it actually was

The ground pour was on layer 4 instead of layer 2. Every fast edge's return
current had to travel through 1.6 mm of FR4 before it found its way home, and
the loop area that created was enough to turn the supply into an antenna, and
the antenna into a feedback path.

> Once you can see the loop, the fix is obvious, and I felt appropriately stupid
> about the three days it took me.

## what I would do differently

- Put the ground plane adjacent to the layer carrying the fast edges. Always. That's the whole rule.
- Check the stack-up before routing, not after fabrication.
- Probe with a short ground spring, not a six-inch clip lead, before concluding anything about high-frequency behaviour.
